Our story

Nine years of building bars
in other people's rooms

Shake&Steer began with one flight case, two bartenders and a stubborn idea: that a cocktail served at a private party should be every bit as good as one served in a great bar.

How it started

One flight case, one wedding, no plan B

The first event was a friend's wedding in a courtyard with no running water and a single power socket. We built a bar out of borrowed crates, carried ice up two flights of stairs and served two hundred and forty drinks.

Nothing about that night was easy, and everything about it was worth repeating. Since then the flight cases have multiplied, the bar has become a modular counter we designed ourselves, and the team has grown — but the working method has not changed. We turn up early, we prepare everything by hand, and we treat somebody's one important evening as exactly that.

Today we pour at weddings in vineyards, birthdays in flats, product launches in galleries and christenings in family houses. The scale changes. The standard does not.

The first season

What makes us different

Four things we refuse to compromise on

Everything made from scratch

Syrups, cordials, shrubs and infusions are produced in our own workshop in small batches, dated and tasted before they leave. No bottled sour mix has ever been on one of our trucks.

Ice treated as an ingredient

We cut our own clear blocks: large cubes for spirit-forward drinks, crushed for juleps, collins spears for highballs. Cold, slow dilution is half the reason a drink still tastes right at the bottom of the glass.

A bar designed for the room

Our counters are modular, and the front is dressed to suit the venue — oak, brushed brass, marble or draped linen. The bar should look like it was always meant to be there.

Service you notice only when you need it

Our bartenders are trained to read a room: quick and quiet during speeches, generous and warm on the dance floor, and never the loudest thing in the photographs.

The experience

What your guests actually feel

A guest walks up to the bar and finds a short, legible menu — four or five drinks chosen for that evening, printed on good paper, with an alcohol-free option that reads as an equal, not an apology.

They are greeted by name if we know it, served in under ninety seconds, and handed a glass that is properly cold, properly balanced and beautiful enough to photograph. Then they go back to the party, which is the entire point.

The people

A small core, and a bench we trust

Every event is led by one of our senior bartenders, who is on site from the first crate to the last box. Around them we build a team sized to your guest list — typically one bartender for every fifty guests, plus a barback and a runner for larger evenings.

Everybody who works a Shake&Steer bar has spent time in a real one. They arrive in uniform, they carry their own tools, and they know that the difference between a good evening and a great one is usually measured in small courtesies.
